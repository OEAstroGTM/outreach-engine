# Intellectible — Voice & Messaging

## Tone
Peer-to-peer. You're a BD person talking to another BD person, not a vendor pitching a buyer. Direct, short, no fluff. The reader is busy and skeptical — earn their time in the first two lines.

## Email Format Rules
- **Plain text only.** No HTML, no images, no formatted bullets.
- **Subject lines:** 3–6 words. No punctuation. Lowercase preferred. No questions.
- **Body:** 3–5 sentences max for a cold email. One ask. One idea per email.
- **CTA:** Soft. "Worth a quick look?" or "Open to a 15-minute call?" — not "Book a demo on my Calendly."
- **Signature:** First name only on cold outreach. Add title and company in follow-ups if needed.

## What Works in GovCon Outreach
- Reference their specific contract vehicle or award history when you know it
- Mention the fiscal year / contract cycle — shows you know the industry rhythm
- Peer language: "BD team," "capture motion," "qualified pipeline," "task orders"
- Be willing to say "I'll stop following up" — it gets replies

## Banned Words and Phrases
- "AI-powered" — sounds like every other pitch
- "revolutionary" / "game-changing" / "cutting-edge"
- "I wanted to reach out" — dead opener
- "Hope this finds you well"
- "leverage" (as a verb)
- "synergies"
- "seamlessly"
- "robust"
- "solutions" (as a standalone noun — say what it actually does)
- "Let me know if you have any questions" — weak close

## Subject Line Patterns That Generated Replies
- Competitor name + contrast: "beyond govtribe"
- State + segment: "FL contractors / FY26"
- Direct problem reference: "contract research time"

## Avoid
- Subject lines that sound like a newsletter
- Opening with "I" (e.g., "I came across your company")
- Asking multiple questions in one email
- Generic social proof ("hundreds of GovCon firms…")
